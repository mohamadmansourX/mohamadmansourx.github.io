Abstract

I build and study machine learning systems, mostly in Beirut. Most of my research has been on explainability: methods that say why a classifier decided what it did, and ways to check whether such explanations can be trusted. Two papers came out of that work with Julia El Zini and Mariette Awad at the American University of Beirut. CEnt searches for the smallest feasible change to an input that flips a model's decision [1]. The second asks whether explanations of sentiment classifiers are faithful to the model and plausible to people [2]. Before that I worked on seizure detection from EEG, with a model that ranks the connectivity features behind each prediction [3].

Other projects covered reinforcement learning for room navigation, text classification for reviews, document image processing, and a telescope for hunting comets, drawn up for the 2023 NASA Space Apps Challenge. I now work at Whish Money on internal AI projects.

Keywords explainable AI, counterfactual explanations, sentiment analysis, EEG, reinforcement learning, computer vision, OCR, PyTorch, TensorFlow

Publications

  1. Julia El Zini, Mohamad Mansour, Mariette Awad.1 CEnt: An Entropy-based Model-agnostic Explainability Framework to Contrast Classifiers’ Decisions arXiv:2301.07941, January 2023.

    Given an input and any classifier, CEnt looks for the smallest feasible change that would flip the decision, while respecting features that cannot change. It fits a decision tree to the model near the input, scores the splits by entropy, turns them into a graph and walks the shortest path to the other side of the boundary. A VAE supplies the distances, so the proposed change stays close to real data. Benchmarked on the adult, COMPAS, Give Me Some Credit and HELOC tabular sets, with image examples on MNIST and Fashion MNIST and a text classifier.

  2. Julia El Zini, Mohamad Mansour, Basel Mousi, Mariette Awad. On the Evaluation of the Plausibility and Faithfulness of Sentiment Analysis Explanations In Artificial Intelligence Applications and Innovations (AIAI 2022), IFIP AICT vol. 647, pp. 338–349. Springer, 2022.

    Two questions about explanations of sentiment classifiers: do the extracted rationales actually drive the prediction (faithfulness), and do they match what people would point to (plausibility)? We collected human rationales for a small dataset and ran LIME, Anchors and SHAP over eight models, from a CNN and bmLSTM to BERT, RoBERTa and XLNet. Anchors sat closest to human judgment, and the transformers were easier to explain than the convolutional and recurrent models.

  3. Mohamad Mansour, Fouad Khnaisser, Hmayag Partamian.1 An Explainable Model for EEG Seizure Detection based on Connectivity Features arXiv:2009.12566, September 2020.

    Connectivity measures between EEG channels, directed and undirected, feed a network that decides whether a short window is seizure activity. Relevance is read off the trained weights and activations, which gives a ranking of the features behind each decision and a way to check it against what is already known about seizures.
